Scenario: assume a starve-fed co-rotating twin-screw compounder for heat-sensitive PHA (exact PHA grade, formulation and operating numbers are unknown). Material escapes from both atmospheric and vacuum vent locations. The discharge contains apparently unmelted particles/lumps. The screw configuration is described as mild, includes kneading blocks, and currently has reverse-conveying elements removed (sometimes one reverse element is used). Raising RPM often alleviates vent discharge but increases the MI/MFR of the processed resin. Decreasing throughput has also increased MI. The operator is unsure whether changing screw layout or increasing melting-zone barrel temperature will help, because a hotter discharged melt can degrade PHA. Treat these as qualitative case observations, not verified measurements or a confirmed degradation mechanism.

Current local guide only lists air/volatiles, local filling, downstream restriction and inadequate melt seal, with generic shutdown/inspection advice. Please resolve these points:
1. Rank useful diagnostic hypotheses conditional on this pattern: incomplete/late melting and nonuniform heating; transport/fill or downstream-pressure limitation; vent-insert carryover; powder/solid entrainment; unstable sealing; moisture/degradation gases. Distinguish an atmospheric upstream/back-vent for solids from a melt-degassing vent: NOT every vent must receive a fully molten polymer. Explain why both atmospheric/vacuum symptoms do not prove a vacuum problem.
2. Explain why higher RPM can improve melting/transport or reduce fill and yet raise final MI; why lower Q can increase SME or time in mixing zones although fill decreases. 3. Answer whether raising early melting-zone barrel setpoints can reduce mechanical melting work without raising exit melt temperature; when it instead worsens damage.
